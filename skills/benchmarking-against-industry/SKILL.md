# Benchmarking against industry

There is no external comparator available here, and none may be supplied from
general knowledge.

## Why not

Two sources can answer a question: the table, which holds what was measured,
and the wiki, which holds this organisation's policy. Neither contains figures
from other organisations. A benchmark recalled from training rather than read
from a source is indistinguishable, in the answer, from one that was verified —
and the reader cannot tell which they were given.

So no figure is offered here, and none should be produced from memory.

## The one comparison that is defined

"Is this good?" has exactly one answerable form in this data: **measured
against this organisation's own resolution targets**, which are policy and do
live in the wiki. Use `computing-target-adherence` for that. It gives a
defensible answer to whether performance meets what was committed to, which is
usually what the question is reaching for.

## What to answer

Give the measured figure, and say that no external comparator exists in either
source, so the figure cannot be placed against one. Where the metric has a
policy target, compare against that instead and say that is what the comparison
is to. Where it has none — and most metrics here have none — report the figure
and state plainly that the data supports no judgement of good or bad.

Internal comparison is still open and is often more useful: the same metric
across periods, priorities, components, or ticket types is all measured, all in
the table, and needs no outside source.
